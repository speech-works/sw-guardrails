# Sources and licences

This skill is Speechworks' own text. Its program rules are drawn from
Speechworks' own `docs/programs/PROGRAM_STRATEGY.md` and the evidence rules in
`ProgramEvidence.ts` (sw-be-2). It draws on these sources, used within
their licences:

- **iso-24495 skill** by Dustin Yuchen Teng (github.com/danyuchn/iso-24495-skill),
  MIT licence. The procedure (read first, lock facts, rewrite, self-check) and
  the four-outcome framing of ISO 24495-1 informed this skill. No text is
  copied.
- **NHS digital service manual, content guide** (service-manual.nhs.uk/content),
  Open Government Licence v3.0. Rules paraphrased: reading age 9 to 11,
  sentences of 20 words at most, paragraphs of about 3 sentences, plain term
  first, natural frequencies for numbers, no metaphors in health content.
- **GOV.UK content design guidance**, Open Government Licence v3.0. Rules
  paraphrased: active voice, words to avoid.
- **US Federal Plain Language Guidelines** (plainlanguage.gov), public domain.
  Rules paraphrased: one idea per sentence, address the reader as "you",
  verbs over nouns.
- **Vale** (vale.sh), MIT licence. Used as the checker. The Speechworks rule
  files in `vale/styles/Speechworks` are original.
- Evidence behind the rules: Nation 2006 (vocabulary coverage), Cooper 1999
  (L2 readers and idioms), Akl et al. 2011 Cochrane review (natural
  frequencies), Redish 2000 and Wang et al. 2013 (limits of readability
  formulas).

ASD-STE100 is a licensed specification. This skill borrows only its general
approach (short sentences, one idea each) and none of its text or dictionary.
