# Approved plain definitions

Use these words when a lesson needs to explain a term or image. They were
checked against the program evidence. If a term is not here, write a
definition, keep it short, and flag it for a clinician before it ships.

| Term or image | Approved plain wording |
|---|---|
| The iceberg | A picture of stuttering. The top of the iceberg is the part other people can hear or see. The larger part under the water is what the person thinks, feels and does to cope, such as fear, shame, beliefs about speaking and avoiding words. It is a way of talking about stuttering that comes from clinical practice. Research has not tested it. |
| Heritability (twin studies) | Twin studies estimate how much of the difference in stuttering between people is linked to genes. Their estimates range from about 40 to 80 in every 100. It describes groups of people. It says nothing about any one person. |
| Safety behaviours | The things people do to get through a hard speaking moment, such as swapping a word, keeping an answer short or avoiding a call. |
| Social anxiety | A strong, lasting fear of being judged by other people, at a level a clinician would diagnose. It is more than ordinary nerves. |
| The alarm | The body's quick response to a threat: a faster heart, tight muscles, shallow breathing. It works fast, before you have time to think. |
